How it works

Four steps, with a person approving every change that touches your network.

  1. Prioritize

    Ranks CVEs in the supported, dated vulnerability catalog using a model assessed against historical vulnerability records. It does not establish that an exploit will occur or that the resulting ranking is optimal for your network.

  2. Decide

    Chooses fix, mitigate, quarantine, escalate or monitor under rules you set, and explains why. Nothing is changed without a signed approval.

  3. Act

    Applies the approved change through a controlled path that validates it first and can put everything back exactly as it was.

  4. Verify

    Confirms the result with evidence the change itself can't influence. If the check fails, the change is rolled back. If it can't see enough, it says "cannot confirm" instead of guessing.

Who it's for

Internet service providers

Detects route announcements a customer isn't entitled to make, applies a prefix filter with approval, and confirms traffic is back on the right path for every router.

Tested on a routed lab network with real routing software: a hijack was detected, contained and verified, and an over-broad filter was caught and rolled back.

Telecom operators

Builds the network map from what devices and switches actually report, and flags equipment and traffic missing from your inventory before it becomes a blind spot.

Tested with SNMPv3 and gNMI over mutual TLS, and on live switch traffic, where it found a host left out of the declared inventory.

Businesses with websites

Tells you which of your vulnerabilities to fix first. When no fix exists yet, it can block just the vulnerable page and keep the rest of your site running.

Tested on a deliberately vulnerable web app: the vulnerable page was blocked, attacks failed, every other page kept working, and the block was lifted once the code was fixed.

What the laboratory tests actually show

Where protocols were pre-registered, we report the original acceptance conditions. We also publish unsuccessful tests, independent verification outcomes, and retrospective audit corrections without describing them as new held-out evidence.

All results come from laboratory networks and public datasets. PREDICT-SHIELD is an experimental prototype for controlled pilots and has not yet been validated on production critical infrastructure. Full reports, code and test records are public on GitHub.
